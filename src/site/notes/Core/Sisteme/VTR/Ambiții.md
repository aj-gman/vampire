Aspirations are the building blocks of the characters, the driving forces that push them forward and animate their every action. They are the main way of gaining flat-out Experiences instead of waiting to accumulate them by the slow accumulation of Beats. Still, they do not have to be something that the character consciously knows[[1\|1]](https://gmantabletops.miraheze.org/wiki/Aspirations#cite_note-1). Aspirations are not set in stone and can be changed at the beginning of every session, reworded or scrapped completely.

> **HOMEBREW:** Aspirations have been deepened in order to provide more narrative drive for players that are mechanically-inclined and want to progress. In this way, resolving Aspirations is a means to maturing fast, which can often result in the crystallization of radical beliefs.

In the [character sheet](https://gmantabletops.miraheze.org/wiki/Terms_%26_Conditions_of_Character_Creation "Terms & Conditions of Character Creation"), Aspirations are spelled out as questions with a certain degree of complexity[[2\|2]](https://gmantabletops.miraheze.org/wiki/Aspirations#cite_note-2), marked by a number from 1 to 5. In order to fulfill an Aspiration, characters must spend an amount of Beats equal to or higher than the question's complexity. After doing so, the player must provide as many Revelations as Beats they have used. Revelations are conclusions the character has expressed or observed during the Chronicle. After doing so, the player can roll the dice to try to answer the Aspiration[[3\|3]](https://gmantabletops.miraheze.org/wiki/Aspirations#cite_note-3) by providing a conclusive reaction that their character has. They roll as many dice as Beats spent. For every success[[4\|4]](https://gmantabletops.miraheze.org/wiki/Aspirations#cite_note-4), they gain 1 Experience[[5\|5]](https://gmantabletops.miraheze.org/wiki/Aspirations#cite_note-5).

> _Mark's Aspiration is related to his struggle with drug use. As such, he has chosen to write it down on the sheet as "Am I strong enough to quit my drug habit?". The Storyteller and him decide it should have a Complexity of 2, needing at least 2 Beats to answer. After a few sessions spent selling drugs and getting a lot of pain dealt to him, he decides he wants to try and answer his Aspiration. He pulls the GM aside in a black-box moment and we narrate a spotlight on his character. Mark is spending 3 Beats to answer this question, providing 3 Revelations: first, he saw the outcome of an overdose, so that's scared him into quitting, secondly, he's had enough interaction with the local police force to know that he is in danger because of his habit, thirdly, he's used his Willpower multiple times to limit the side effects of his addiction. He rolls 3d10 and he gets two Hits, meaning he receives 2 Experiences and removes the Aspiration, deciding that, if need be, he will force himself to go cold turkey. Immediately, he writes down a new Aspiration: "Is it necessary to quit my drug habit?". They decide the Complexity of this Aspiration should be 3, thus requiring at least 3 Beats to answer._

### Additional Notes

1. As suggested in the VTR:2e Rulebook, they can be "meta", some goal that the character isn't aware of but the player is.
2. This is a mechanic reinterpreted from [The Silt Verses RPG](https://www.gauntlet-rpg.com/the-silt-verses-roleplaying-game.html), in which the Assignments can only be completed by answering Questions.
3. Not all Aspirations need to be positive. Players may start out with noble Aspirations and find themselves betrayed at every corner, so Revelations can spell out a different answer than they expect. This is good!
4. Sometimes, there are no successes, and the player goes back to the drawing board. This is realistic: not every personal breakthrough actually results in maturity and growth.
5. Of course, this is a risky gamble. Statistically, there's little chance for Hits to actually happen, but the potential payout is quite high: for every Beat spent, you have a 1-in-3 chance of getting 1 Experience, the equivalent of 5 Beats.
